# Candidates — Real-Life Patch Notes: The Comedy Changelog

Gate: one single-line patch note / changelog entry for a bug in real life or
human biology, 1–160 characters, in the terse register of a real release
note — version number, engineering voice, the fix stated as if it shipped.

Judged in the bounty's own order:

1. A single-line patch note for a real-life or human-biology bug, and reads
   as comedy.
2. Genuinely funny on first read — a real comedic idea, not just the word
   "bug" dropped into a sentence.
3. Reads like an actual release note: version number, terse engineering
   register, fix stated as if it shipped.
4. Names a specific, recognisable bug of the body or daily life — the
   reader winces and laughs at once.
5. Uses its 160 characters well: no padding, every word working.

## Duels

**7 vs 1** — Both name the most recognisable bug in daily life: memory
failing at the exact moment it is needed. 1's doorway amnesia is the more
famous symptom, and "holding a mug is now optional" lands well. But 7 has
the stronger comedic mechanism: a real engineering term, "memory leak,"
doing honest double duty, and a hard contrast with numbers in it — a song
heard once in 2007 kept *permanently* against a grocery list expiring in
90 seconds. The wince is sharper because both halves are true at once.
7 wins on criteria 2, 4 and 5.

**7 vs 13** — 13's 3 a.m. replay of a 2014 embarrassment is a genuine
wince-and-laugh, but its register drifts toward a joke about sleep rather
than a shipped fix, and the bug (rumination) is less concrete than 7's
storage asymmetry. 7 wins on criteria 3 and 4.

**7 vs 3** — 3 has the best pure body-wince in the slate (bubble-wrap
knees after 30) and a classic release-note gag in "this is now a feature."
But the gag is a familiar template, while 7's joke is built out of the
bug itself. 7 wins on criterion 2.

**7 vs 4** — 4 is funny, but low-resolution dreams are a spec complaint
more than a bug, and the avoided-person kicker wanders from the fix. 7
stays on one bug for its whole length. 7 wins on criteria 1 and 5.

**1 vs 13 (for runner-up)** — Both are universal. 13's laugh depends on
the reader supplying their own 2014 conversation; 1 names the exact
physical scene — doorway, room, mug — so the recognition is instant and
the optional-mug kicker pays it off in-note. 1 takes runner-up on
criteria 2 and 4.

The rest fall earlier: 2 and 12 are vivid but single-gag; 5 and 6 list
their jokes instead of landing one; 8's queue joke is cute but the bug is
minor; 9 overlaps 1's everyday-forgetting theme and is weaker; 10 spends
its length on the meeting aside; 11 and 14 are recognisable but quiet —
they raise a smile, not a laugh.
